Ouémé–Ogun Recharge Explorer

How reliable is this?

  • Rainfall products disagree. WaPOR v3's precipitation layer is CHIRPS v3.0 (identical values), which is than CHIRPS v2.0. All three estimates are shown rather than picking one.
  • Net, not gross, recharge. Satellite ET already includes water that plants and valley bottoms draw from groundwater, so rainfall − ET − quick flow is recharge minus groundwater evaporation. Wells measure gross recharge. The two should differ.
  • ET uncertainty is large. WaPOR v2 errors across Africa were about 29–31% (Blatchford et al., 2020); v3 has not been evaluated in these catchments.
  • No observed river flow for 2018–2024 was available. The expected flow uses the observed Bétérou runoff ratios for 1971–1987 and 1988–2010, assuming the ratio has not changed. No flow data were found for the Upper Ogun; its band is an assumption.
  • Validation against wells is pending. AMMA-CATCH groundwater-level data (registration required) are needed for the water-table fluctuation comparison; the code is ready (scripts/04_validate_wtf.py).
  • Scale. Catchment means over ~10,000 km² hide local differences; maps are at 5 km.

Data

  • Rainfall: CHIRPS v2.0 and v3.0 annual totals (Climate Hazards Center, 0.05°, 1981–2024) and TAMSAT v3.1 monthly totals (University of Reading, 0.0375°, gap-filled estimate). WaPOR v3's own precipitation layer equals CHIRPS v3.0.
  • Evapotranspiration: FAO WaPOR v3 Level 1 actual ET and interception (300 m), and reference ET (~30 km), 2018–2024. CC BY 4.0.
  • Catchments: HydroBASINS v1c level 9. Upper Ouémé outlet at Bétérou (unit 1090852360); Upper Ogun above the Oyan confluence (unit 1090928900).
  • Observed river flow at Bétérou: 47.5 m³/s (1971–1987) and 54.5 m³/s (1988–2010), Proc. IAHS 383, 163–169 (2020).

Method

  1. Area-weighted catchment means of each layer for each year.
  2. Surplus = rainfall − ET, for each of the three rainfall estimates.
  3. Closure check (Upper Ouémé): over several years, surplus − expected river flow should be near zero. Expected flow = observed Bétérou runoff ratio × rainfall; the higher of the two observed ratios is used, which gives the smaller residual.
  4. Because the residual is far from zero: how much ET would have to rise, rainfall fall, or the runoff ratio change to close it, and how far the water table would have had to rise if the residual were stored underground (specific yield 0.2–1.5%, Lachassagne et al., 2021).
  5. Comparison with published recharge from groundwater levels in Benin crystalline rock: about 4% of rainfall from 19–25-year records in weathered crystalline rock (Kotchoni et al., 2019), and 5–24% from two years of water-table data in the Donga sub-basin of the Upper Ouémé (Kamagaté et al., 2007). These give scale; they are not a validation.
